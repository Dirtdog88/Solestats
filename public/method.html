SoleStats

How the index works

SoleStats asks AI assistants the questions runners ask before buying shoes, many times over, and scores what comes back: which brands get recommended, whether the facts are right, what gets cited, and where the sale goes. It is an independent project. Scores describe AI answers on the dates sampled, not the products and not the brand. Brands get their numbers a few days before each edition.

Reading the tables

Every number is a rate across repeated answers, never a single answer. The ± beside a score is how much it moved between repeats of the same prompts; gaps under about five points are noise. The brand table sorts by Overall; a dash means the subject has no data for that brand in this edition. The "Where the sale goes" table is the share of answers in each category that point to each place to buy. The engine cards show how many answers each engine gave, how many ran a web search, how many named their sources, how many searched without naming any, and how many answered without searching.

A yellow banner marks a preview: a partial or sample round whose numbers are not findings.

Downloads

Every prompt, every answer with its citations, every detected mention and route, and the scored table are free to download as CSV from the front page. Raw API responses are kept but not published.

What we ask

138 prompts written the way runners type them: 102 about shoes (grouped by need: starting out, budget, cushion, stability and injuries, fit, racing, trail, drop, conditions, specific people), 28 head-to-head questions between the eight biggest shoe brands, and 8 about where to buy. Outside the head-to-head set, no prompt names a brand. The full list is in the downloads.

Who we ask

Three AI assistants through their APIs with web search turned on, so answers resemble the consumer apps and carry citations. Each prompt is asked three times per engine. A full round runs every month. Accurate's spec questions and the hand checks behind Accurate and Shoppable run once a quarter; in the months between, the scores use the latest checks, and each brand page says which round they come from. The front page names the engines and model ids in the current edition; an engine marked pending is being added and its answers join the same edition when they are in. We never present a single answer as a finding; everything is a rate across repeats, with a margin that shows how much it moved.

How shoes are scored

  • Recommended (35%): how often the brand is named on prompts where it has a credible shoe for that need, plus its head-to-head record, how often it is the first pick, and how consistent that is across repeats.
  • Accurate (25%): seven facts per model (current version, price, weight, drop, stack band, widths, category) checked against the brand's own US site on a recorded date. "Not sure" scores the same as wrong.
  • Shoppable by AI (20%): the share of a twelve-item checklist the brand's US site passes, checked by hand once per round: AI crawlers allowed in robots.txt; schema.org Product data with offers; price, widths, stock and specs readable without scripts; the current version named clearly; returns and shipping findable within two links; crawlable pages for stability, neutral, trail, racing and wide; a path to cart and checkout without a login wall or bot block; a store locator. The full list with how each item is checked is in the downloads as the checklist CSV.
  • Sourced (10%): what AI cites when it talks about the brand: the brand's site, established review sites, forums, retailers or junk. Only sources an answer names count. Two kinds of answer are counted beside the score instead: source not provided, where the engine searched the web but named none of what it found (Claude's current search tool works this way), and no search, where it answered from what it already knew (Gemini decides for itself whether to search, and mostly does not). Both are real ways shoppers get answers, so they still count for Recommended and Where the sale goes. The pages a search returned are in the downloads. Another brand's site does not count in its favour. A brand's own sites include its outlet stores and its sites outside the US, which count the same and are shown separately. With fewer than five answers that name a source, Sourced is not scored and Overall is weighted over the other subjects.
  • Where the sale goes (10%): whether answers that name the brand send the shopper to the brand's site or run specialty, or elsewhere. Only the brand's own site counts as its site: a link to another brand's site is not a place to buy this brand's shoes and is left out. A general line such as "buy from the brand's own website" counts for every brand the answer names.

Scores are 0 to 100 with no curve. Eighteen brands are watched: the eight biggest plus ten more scored on the prompts where they have a credible shoe, as recorded in the verified spec sheet. A brand with no verified models yet is scored on every shoe prompt, and its card says so.

Revenue

Each brand page shows the latest revenue the brand, or the company that owns it, has reported publicly: from its 10-K, 20-F, annual report or official results release, in the currency it reports in, with the change from the year before and a link to the source. Where the owner does not break the brand out, the figure is labelled as the footwear, segment or whole-company number it is. Private companies are shown only with figures they have announced themselves. Revenue is context for the size of each brand; it is not scored, and no link between revenue and the AI scores is claimed. The figures are refreshed once a quarter and are in the downloads.

What it can't tell you

AI answers change week to week and engine to engine. A score describes the answers we sampled on the dates given, not the products and not the brand. Eighteen brands and one round cannot support any claim about sales; we show search interest and public scale figures beside the scores only as context. Detection of brand names, citations and retailers is automated and then reviewed by hand; the raw answers are downloadable so anyone can check.

Run it on your own category

The prompt templates, the rubric, the runner script and the scoring code are open. Swap the brand list and the prompts and the same pipeline scores any category.
